# Glossary , mse435

One meaning per term. The lesson uses the term exactly this way.

- adoption , share of potential users or firms that actually use a
  technology in a period.
- average total cost , total cost divided by quantity, TC / Q.
- batching , grouping multiple inference requests so one model pass
  serves them together.
- bottleneck , the stage or resource that limits total throughput of
  a system.
- break-even , the volume at which revenue equals total cost.
- build/lease , the choice between owning a data center and renting
  capacity from a provider.
- capex , capital expenditure: one-time spend on long-lived assets.
- capacity , the maximum quantity a system can supply per period.
- complements , goods used together, so a price fall in one raises
  demand for the other.
- consumer AI , AI products sold to individuals, priced per user or
  free with ads.
- contribution margin , price minus variable cost per unit, P - v.
- cooling , removal of waste heat from chips, by air or liquid.
- custom accelerator , a chip designed for one workload class,
  such as an ASIC or TPU.
- data governance , rules for who may access data, where it lives,
  and how long it is kept.
- depreciation , accounting spread of an asset's cost over its
  useful life.
- discount rate , the rate that converts future dollars to present
  dollars.
- enterprise AI , AI products sold to firms, priced per seat, per
  task, or by contract.
- equilibrium , the price and quantity where quantity demanded
  equals quantity supplied.
- fixed cost , cost that does not change with quantity in the
  period.
- forecast , a forward-looking claim about an unknown quantity,
  always uncertain.
- gigawatt scale , data-center builds at or above 1 GW of power,
  roughly a large power plant.
- GPU economy , the market for GPU compute: supply, price, and
  allocation of accelerators.
- inference , running a trained model to produce outputs.
- interconnect , the network links between chips and servers that
  move activations and gradients.
- long contract , a multi-year commit to buy compute or tokens at
  set terms.
- marginal cost , the cost of producing one more unit.
- memory bottleneck , the state where memory bandwidth, not compute,
  limits speed.
- NPV , net present value: present value of cash inflows minus
  outflows.
- opex , operating expenditure: recurring spend to run the business.
- open/closed stack , whether model weights, APIs, and infra are
  freely usable or vendor-controlled.
- productivity , output per unit of input, such as per worker hour.
- PUE , power usage effectiveness: total facility power divided by
  IT equipment power.
- scenario analysis , recomputation of outcomes under a small set of
  named alternative assumptions.
- sensitivity , how much an outcome moves when one input moves.
- service-as-software , the thesis that AI turns labor-like
  services into software-priced products.
- substitutes , goods that replace each other, so a price fall in
  one lowers demand for the other.
- supply concentration , the share of supply held by few producers.
- token , the billing unit of LLM input and output text.
- token demand , quantity of tokens buyers want at a price.
- tornado chart , a bar chart that ranks inputs by their effect on
  an outcome.
- training , fitting model weights on data, a one-time large
  compute cost.
- uncertainty , the range of plausible outcomes around a forecast.
- unit price , price per billing block, such as dollars per 1M
  tokens.
- utilization , used capacity divided by total capacity.
- value capture , the share of total created value that a firm
  keeps as profit.
- value chain , the ordered layers from raw input to final product,
  here chips to applications.
- variable cost , cost that rises with quantity in the period.
- vendor lock-in , the cost of leaving a vendor once systems depend
  on it.
- cache hit rate , the share of requests served from cache, h.
- clean room , an isolated environment where proprietary data is
  processed without leaking to shared models.
- cost per success , expected cost of one successful task,
  (r+1) x c / success.
- data flywheel , the loop where usage corrects data, corrections
  raise quality, and quality raises usage.
- escalation rate , the share of AI outputs sent to human review,
  e.
- falsifiable , stated so that a named measurement can prove it
  wrong.
- fixed cost floor , the minimum cost set by infrastructure that
  does not scale with volume.
- guardrail , a pre-registered limit that blocks launch or forces
  rollback when breached.
- hit rate , see cache hit rate.
- human escalation , see escalation rate.
- kill criteria , the named conditions under which a pilot or
  project stops.
- pre-register , to fix the decision rule, threshold, and duration
  before seeing the data.
- ramp , the assumed adoption path over time, such as
  [0.20, 0.50, 0.80].
- review labor , human time spent checking AI output, priced per
  task as tasks x e x minutes/60 x wage.
- risk reserve , the budget cushion K added to TCO for surprises.
- rollback , restoring the previous working system, here required
  within 15 minutes.
- shadow mode , running the new system on live traffic without
  affecting users.
- success rate , the share of tasks completed correctly, s.
- switching cost , the one-time cost of moving from one vendor or
  design to another.
- TCO , total cost of ownership: build + 3 x (run + labor) +
  risk reserve.
- trust boundary , the line across which data may move only under
  contract.
- verification gap , the cost difference between generating an
  output and checking it.
